My Academic and Professional Career

Academics

I received both my undergraduate and graduate degrees from the New Mexico Institute of Mining and Technology. During my time in the undergraduate Computer Science program, I learned basic concepts of programming like data structures and proper formatting to more complex subjects like algorithm design and compiler writing. My education was both extensive and varied, and I came away knowing Python, C, and Java, with some foundational skills in other languages like JavaScript, SQL, and C#. Some of my favorite courses during my undergraduate program were: Algorithms and Data Structures, Software Engineering, and Video Game Design. My capstone for the program was writing a compiler for a toy version of Java.

My graduate program allowed me to explore more complex areas of my field. This included diving further into computational theory with complexity classes, as well as getting my hands dirty with machine learning and data science classes. All of this experience cumulated in my thesis in educational technology.

After being out of school for a few years, I decided to keep myself sharp by completing the Data Analysis and Backend Development course programs on Boot.dev (you can see my profile here). The experience taught me a lot about more specific technologies than what I got in my formal education (like getting to mess around in PostgresSQL, Docker, and RabbitMQ), and it's also where I learned Go and Typescript.

Professional Life

After graduating in 2023, I went to work for the NMT First Responders Training Program as a Software Developer. In this role, I am mainly responsible for identifying places in our team's workflow that are bottlenecking our proceedures and coming up with user-friendly, lightweight bits of software to help make the whole process more efficient. Another of my primary responsibilities has been working in conjunction with contractors to maintain and improve our digital infrastructure. This includes our front-facing websites, as well as our internal applications. This role has taught me a lot about working as a part of a team and interfacing with other professionals both in and outside of my field. It's also taught me a lot about how to interface with customers and end users of our products and how to use those interactions to help better their experiences.

Outside of my job, I try to use my skills to improve my community where I can. For example, I go to my local games store to play Magic: The Gathering most weeks. I noticed the owner's system for placing people in play groups seemed a bit tiring, so I offered to build him a program to help smooth out the process. After a few conversations, we landed on an idea for an embeded application in Discord (a bot for anyone familiar) that he could use to enter his participant list and place people automatically. You can see that project here. It was fulfilling to be able to give back in some way to my community, and I hope to continue the practice in the future.
